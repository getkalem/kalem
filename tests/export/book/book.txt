1 Foundations
=============

1.1 Why plain text
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  Books have been typeset from plain text for decades (Knuth, Donald E.,
  1984).  Org keeps the structure of a document in its text: headings,
  lists, tables and formulas are all written as characters on a line, so
  the file outlives every program that edits it.[1]


1.1.1 A pipeline
----------------

  The text goes through one exporter for each format, as 1 shows: HTML
  for the web, LaTeX for print, and Word through pandoc for colleagues
  who ask for it (Dominik, Carsten, 2010).

  <file:figures/pipeline.png>

  The code that builds the book can live in the book itself, as in
  literate programming (Schulte, Eric and Davison, Dan and Dye, Thomas
  and Dominik, Carsten, 2012):

  ,----
  | kalem export book.org --to html
  | kalem export book.org --to pdf
  | kalem export book.org --to docx
  `----
  Listing 1: Building every format.


1.2 Measurements
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

  1 lists how long each export of this sample took, and the total is
  computed by the table itself. The energy of a body at rest, equation
  ???, is the usual example of a displayed formula.[2]

   Format  First run  Second run  Mean 
  -------------------------------------
   HTML           12          10    11 
   LaTeX          15          13    14 
   Word          420         400   410 
  -------------------------------------
   Total         447         423   435 
  Table 1: Export times of the sample, in milliseconds.

  \begin{equation}
  E = m c^2
  \end{equation}

  Inline mathematics works too: the mean of $n$ runs is $\bar{x} =
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$. Back in [the first chapter], the
  pipeline of 1.1.1 produced these files.
