#+TITLE: LaTeX as written: a specification

TeX has a reference implementation and a book, and LaTeX has a kernel
and its documentation, but neither has a specification of the language
documents are written in: which characters mean what, what a command
takes as its arguments, how a document is numbered, what a reader of
the source must understand to show it as it reads. Every program other
than TeX that reads LaTeX (converters, editors, checkers) has made its
own guess. This appendix writes down what the Kalem project established
while making its LaTeX mode read real documents, so that the guess need
not be made again. It is descriptive of LaTeX 2ε as authors write it and
normative for a program that claims to read it as this appendix does.

It is independent of Kalem: it says what a document means, not how an
editor shows it. Where Kalem's own choices are meant, Part II's
[[file:../part-2/latex.org][LaTeX]] chapter is the reference.

* Status and scope

This is version 1 of the specification, of 2026-10. It covers:

- documents for the LaTeX 2ε kernel with TeX's standard category codes,
  compiled by pdfTeX, XeTeX or LuaTeX;
- the standard classes (=article=, =report=, =book=), the AMS classes,
  =memoir= and the KOMA-Script classes as far as numbering goes, and the
  class files of journals that keep LaTeX's commands (=revtex4-2=,
  =elsarticle=, =IEEEtran=, =acmart=, =llncs=);
- the packages most papers load: =amsmath=, =amssymb=, =amsthm=,
  =graphicx=, =hyperref=, =cleveref=, =natbib=, =biblatex= (its citation
  commands), =xcolor=, =booktabs=, =multirow=, =subcaption=, =subfig=,
  =enumitem=, =siunitx= (its number and unit commands), =glossaries= and
  =acronym= (their uses), =xspace=, =pifont=, =tikz= (as pictures).

It does not cover TeX's macro language in general. A document that
changes category codes, defines its commands with =\csname= loops or
expands macros to build its structure is outside it; §12 says what a
reader does with such parts.

* Conventions

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are used as in
RFC 2119.

- A /reader/ is a program that takes LaTeX source and gives its structure:
  paragraphs, commands with their arguments, environments, formulas,
  sections with their numbers, labels with what they point at.
- A /renderer/ is a reader that also shows the text as it is typeset
  (an editor's view, a converter's output).
- /Typesets as/ means: pdfLaTeX with the document's preamble puts the
  same glyphs in the same order in a box (the test of §13.2).
- /Blank/ means a space or a tab; /line ending/ means LF, CRLF or a lone CR.

Examples are LaTeX source in a fixed-width font; the arrow ⇒ gives what
it typesets or means.

* Basis

Every rule below is backed by at least one of:

1. *pdfLaTeX.* Generated documents compiled by pdfLaTeX, whose boxes
   (=\showbox=) or auxiliary files are compared with what the rule
   predicts: one line per box for text (§13.2), the =.aux= file's labels
   for numbering (§13.3). The generators are random and seeded; their
   seeds run in continuous integration, a new seed on every run.
2. *A corpus.* 925 papers submitted to arXiv in 2024, 179 to 189 per
   field in biology, computer science, economics, mathematics and
   physics (the first of each category by date with a LaTeX source, the
   categories of a field in equal shares), 2,496 files read by their documents, 86 MB of text between
   =\begin{document}= and =\end{document}=. §11 gives the measures.
3. *The documented behaviour* of the kernel and the packages
   (=source2e=, the package manuals), where 1 and 2 agree with it.

* Characters and tokens

** Category codes

A reader MUST read the source with TeX's standard category codes:

| Characters                 | Meaning                                                    |
|----------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| =\=                        | escape: a control sequence follows                         |
| ={= =}=                    | begin and end of a group                                   |
| =$=                        | math shift                                                 |
| =&=                        | alignment tab                                              |
| =#=                        | macro parameter                                            |
| =^= =_=                    | superscript, subscript (in math)                           |
| =%=                        | comment: the rest of the line, its line ending included    |
| =~=                        | an unbreakable space                                       |
| the letters a to z, A to Z | letters                                                    |
| everything else            | other characters, typeset as themselves                    |

=@= is a letter between =\makeatletter= and =\makeatother= and other
characters elsewhere. A reader MUST NOT follow other category code
changes (=\catcode=, =\ExplSyntaxOn=, active characters); it MAY report
them.

** Control sequences

A /control word/ is =\= followed by one or more letters; it ends at the
first character that is not a letter. A /control symbol/ is =\= followed
by any one other character, a line ending counting as a character (it
reads as a control space).

*Blanks after a control word.* The blanks after a control word are not
text: TeX skips them while reading the name. A single line ending among
them is skipped too; a blank line is a paragraph break and is not.

#+begin_src latex
\LaTeX is   fine   ⇒ LaTeXis fine
\LaTeX{} is fine   ⇒ LaTeX is fine
\LaTeX\ is fine    ⇒ LaTeX is fine
#+end_src

Blanks after a control symbol are text: =\%= followed by two blanks and
=x= typesets =% x=, with one space (runs of blanks are one space, §5.1).

** Lines and paragraphs

A line ending is a space in text. One or more blank lines (lines of only
blanks) end a paragraph, as =\par= does. Blanks at the start of a line
are skipped. A comment removes the rest of its line and its line ending,
so =a%= at the end of a line joins it to the next with no space.

* Text

** Spaces

Runs of blanks and a single line ending typeset one interword space.
Spaces at the start of a paragraph are dropped. =~= is an interword
space that does not break. The control space, a backslash followed by a blank, is an interword
space that is not skipped after a control word.

| Source                   | Typesets as                         |
|--------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| =\,= =\thinspace=        | a thin space (1/6 em)               |
| =\:= =\>= =\;=           | medium and thick spaces (in text with amsmath: as in math) |
| =\!=                     | a negative thin space               |
| =\enspace= =\quad= =\qquad= | ½ em, 1 em, 2 em                 |
| =\@= =\/= =\-=           | nothing visible (spacing and hyphenation hints) |
| =\space=                 | a space                             |
| =\relax=                 | nothing                             |

*=\ignorespaces= expands.* =\ignorespaces= skips the blanks after it and
expands macros while it skips, so a macro that expands to a space (=\space=)
is skipped too. xcolor's =\color{c}= ends with it:

#+begin_src latex
A{\color{blue} \space x}  ⇒ Ax
#+end_src

** Ligatures and quotes

In the text fonts of LaTeX's standard encodings (OT1, T1) the characters
below are ligatures, made by the font:

| Source  | Typesets as |
|---------+-------------|
| =``=    | “           |
| =''=    | ”           |
| =`=     | ‘           |
| ='=     | ’           |
| =--=    | –           |
| =---=   | —           |
| =?`=    | ¿           |
| =!`=    | ¡           |
| =,,= (T1) | „         |
| =<<= =>>= (T1) | « » |

A ligature forms only between characters in the same font with nothing
between them: =-{}-= and =-\relax-= typeset two hyphens. Whether two
quotes join into one ligature depends on what boxes are between them.
The typewriter fonts have no ligatures of dashes and quotes in T1.

** Accents and text symbols

The accent commands (=\'= =\`= =\^= =\"= =\~= =\== =\.= =\u= =\v= =\H=
=\c= =\k= =\r= =\b= =\d= =\t=) take one argument, a group or a single
token: =\'e= =\'{e}= =\' e= all typeset é. =\i= and =\j= are the dotless
letters for accenting. The named letters (=\ss= =\ae= =\AE= =\oe= =\OE=
=\o= =\O= =\aa= =\AA= =\l= =\L=) and the text symbols (=\textohm=,
=\texteuro=, =\textdegree=, =\textsection=, …) typeset one character.
With =\textohm= that character is U+2126 OHM SIGN; it is not changed by
a case change.

** Case changes

| Command                         | What it changes                                                       |
|---------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =\MakeUppercase= =\MakeLowercase= | LaTeX's: expands its argument first, then changes the case of letters, accented letters and the named letters (=\ss= ⇒ SS) |
| =\uppercase= =\lowercase=       | TeX's primitives: change the character codes of the letters in their argument as written, without expanding it |

=\romannumeral n= typesets =n= in lower-case Roman numerals (=\romannumeral 4= ⇒
iv; nothing for 0), so =\MakeUppercase{\romannumeral 4}= ⇒ IV.

A reader SHOULD NOT nest TeX's =\lowercase= around LaTeX's case change of
an accented letter: =\lowercase{\MakeLowercase{é}}= does not end in
pdfLaTeX.

** Fonts and colors

The text commands (=\textbf=, =\textit=, =\emph=, =\texttt=, =\textsc=,
=\textsf=, =\textrm=, =\textsl=, =\textup=, =\textmd=, =\textnormal=,
=\underline=, =\textsuperscript=, =\textsubscript=) take one argument and
style it. The declarations (=\bfseries= =\itshape= =\em= =\ttfamily=
=\scshape= =\sffamily= =\normalfont=, the old =\bf= =\it= =\tt= =\sc=
=\sf= =\rm=, and the sizes =\tiny= … =\Huge=, =\normalsize=) style the
rest of their group: to the =}= that closes it, to the end of the
environment, or, outside both, to the end of the paragraph. =\emph=
inside italic text is upright.

=\textcolor{c}{x}= colors =x=; =\color{c}= colors the rest of its group
and skips the blanks after it (§5.1). =\textcolor[model]{spec}{x}= and
=\color[model]{spec}= give the color by a model (=rgb=, =RGB=, =HTML=,
=gray=, =cmyk=).

* Groups and arguments

** Groups

={…}= is a group: declarations inside it end at its =}=. A group MAY span
paragraph breaks. An environment's body is a group.

** Arguments

A command takes its arguments by a /signature/, a string of letters read
left to right:

| Letter | Argument                                                                                         |
|--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =s=, =*= | an optional star, immediately after what precedes it                                           |
| =o=    | an optional argument: =[…]= with a matching =]=, braces balanced inside, before the end of the paragraph |
| =O{d}= | an optional argument with the default =d=                                                         |
| =m=    | a mandatory argument: a group, or else a single token                                             |

Before each argument the reader skips blanks and at most one line
ending. A mandatory argument that is not a group is one token: a control
word with the blanks after it, a control symbol, or one character
(=\frac12= ⇒ ½: two arguments, =1= and =2=). An optional argument that is
not there leaves the =[= or the text as text.

The signatures of the kernel's and the common packages' commands are
those of their documentation; Part II's [[file:../part-2/latex.org][LaTeX]] chapter lists the ones a
reader most needs. A command whose signature is not known takes no
arguments: =\foo{a}{b}= is the command =\foo= and two groups, which keeps
what follows it readable.

** Verbatim arguments

=\verb|…|= (any delimiter but a blank, a letter or =*=; =\verb*= shows
spaces) and =\lstinline= take their text as written, to the delimiter,
on one line. =\url{…}= and the address of =\href{…}{text}= are verbatim
in a group, so =%=, =#=, =_= and =~= in them are not special. The
environments =verbatim=, =verbatim*=, =Verbatim=, =BVerbatim=,
=lstlisting=, =minted=, =comment=, =filecontents= end at the first
literal =\end{name}=; nothing before it is a command, a comment or a
group.

* Documents and projects

** Preamble and body

A document is =\documentclass[options]{class}=, a /preamble/ of
definitions and =\usepackage= lines, then =\begin{document}= … =\end{document}=,
the /body/. What follows =\end{document}= is ignored. A file without
=\begin{document}= is a part of a document (§7.2).

** The root document of a file

A file belongs to the document that reads it. A reader MUST find it in
this order:

1. a =% !TEX root = file= line among the first lines;
2. =\documentclass[main.tex]{subfiles}= (the subfiles package);
3. the file itself, when it has =\documentclass=;
4. a document with =\documentclass= in the file's folder or a folder
   above it, within the project, that includes the file (directly or
   through other files);
5. any document of the project that includes it.

** Inclusion

| Command                          | Reads                                                                                       |
|----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =\input{f}=, =\input f=          | =f= or =f.tex=, relative to the root document's folder (=\input f= reads the name to a blank) |
| =\include{f}=                    | =f.tex=, on a new page, when =\includeonly= does not leave it out                          |
| =\subfile{f}=                    | =f.tex=, whose own preamble is ignored inside the root                                     |
| =\import{dir}{f}=, =\subimport=  | =f= in =dir=, later inputs relative to it (the import package)                             |

A local =.sty= or =.cls= file in the document's folder is part of it:
its definitions (§8) are the document's.

A file need not be UTF-8: =\usepackage[latin1]{inputenc}= and
=[applemac]= files are common in older papers. A reader MUST read such a
file's commands and definitions all the same (its letters outside ASCII
decoded in its encoding, or replaced); a file it cannot decode MUST NOT
lose the project its definitions.

* Definitions

** Commands

| Form                                                   | Defines                                                                 |
|--------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =\newcommand{\c}[n]{body}= (also =\renewcommand=, =\providecommand=, starred) | =\c= with =n= (0–9) mandatory arguments =#1= … =#n=        |
| =\newcommand{\c}[n][d]{body}=                          | the first argument optional, =d= when it is not given                   |
| =\newcommandtwoopt{\c}[n][d1][d2]{body}= (twoopt)       | the first two arguments optional                                        |
| =\def\c#1#2{body}= (also =\gdef=, =\edef=, =\xdef=)    | =\c= with the parameters written; =\edef= expands the body now          |
| ~\let\c=\d~, ~\let\c\d~                                | =\c= as =\d= is now                                                     |
| =\DeclareRobustCommand=                                | as =\newcommand=                                                        |
| =\NewDocumentCommand{\c}{spec}{body}= (xparse; also =\RenewDocumentCommand=, =\ProvideDocumentCommand=, =\DeclareDocumentCommand=) | =\c= with the arguments of =spec= (§6.2's letters) |
| =\DeclareMathOperator{\op}{text}=                      | =\op= as the operator =text= (upright, operator spacing)                |
| =\newenvironment{e}[n][d]{begin}{end}=                 | the environment =e=                                                     |

*Expansion is at the use.* A body is a template; it is expanded where
the command is used, with the definitions in force there. A definition
MAY use a command defined after it (=\newcommand{\vu}{\vc{u}}= before
=\vc= is fine). A label, citation or section inside a body counts only
where the command is used.

*Arguments of the document's own commands.* A reader that does not
expand macros MUST NOT give the document's commands signatures for
parsing, but a renderer SHOULD use their definitions: a command whose
body, its arguments put in, is text typesets that text
(=\newcommand{\vs}[2]{#1 versus #2}=, =\vs{A}{B}= ⇒ A versus B); a command
whose body is its first argument alone or in one style or color
(={#1}=, =\textbf{#1}=, ={\color{black}{#1}}=) typesets its argument.
The same holds for bodies of other kinds, read as TeX reads them in
running text:

- a reference (=\newcommand{\eq}{\eqref}=, =\newcommand{\rife}[1]{(\ref{#1})}=)
  prints what the reference prints;
- space between lines (=\vspace{2mm}=, =\vskip.075in=) prints nothing in
  the line;
- a formula (=\ensuremath{…}=, text with =$…$= in it, =\(…\)=) is a
  formula; a displayed formula around the arguments
  (=\begin{align}#1\end{align}=, =\[#1\]=) is a displayed formula;
- of =\ifmmode A\else B\fi= only =B= counts, the text-mode branch;
- a box around the text (=\scalebox{0.7}[#1]{…}=, =\resizebox=,
  =\raisebox=) is its text;
- a body that draws pictures or reads files (=\includegraphics=, =\input=
  inside a =figure= or =minipage=) shows those pictures and names those
  files, with its caption.

*=\xspace=* (the xspace package) at the end of a body adds a space unless
punctuation or a closing brace follows.

*What a reader MUST NOT redefine.* A document or package may redefine
TeX's own definition commands (=\def=, =\newcommand=, =\begin=, =\left=,
=\relax=, …) internally. A reader that collects definitions MUST NOT take
such a redefinition: it would change every definition after it.

** Equation macros

Papers define macros for the opening and closing of a displayed
equation: =\def\be{\begin{equation}}=, =\def\ee{\end{equation}}=. A reader
MUST treat a macro whose body opens (closes) a display math environment
as that opening (closing), so the formula between is math. The pairs
~\be~ ~\ee~, ~\beq~ ~\eeq~, ~\beqn~ ~\eeqn~, ~\bea~ ~\eea~,
~\beqa~ ~\eeqa~, ~\beqna~ ~\eeqna~, ~\bes~ ~\ees~, ~\ben~ ~\een~ and
~\begeq~ ~\endeq~ are so common that a reader SHOULD take them as such
when the file does not define them (a file of a project whose root does).
=\begin{equation}= … =\ee= is the same environment.

Such macros are often defined in another file of the project: a package
of the document's own (=\def \ba {\begin{eqnarray}}= in =macros.sty=) or a
file of definitions the root reads. A reader MUST parse each file of a
project with the equation macros and formula environments that the
project's files define (the root document's folder and the folders in
it, =.tex=, =.sty= and =.cls= files), the file's own definitions after
them.

* Structure and numbering

** Classes

| Class kind | Classes                                 | Chapters | =secnumdepth= | Counters reset by =chapter=                |
|------------+-----------------------------------------+----------+---------------+--------------------------------------------|
| book       | =book= =scrbook= =extbook=              | yes      | 2             | =equation= =figure= =table= =footnote=     |
| report     | =report= =scrreprt= =extreport=         | yes      | 2             | =equation= =figure= =table= =footnote=     |
| amsbook    | =amsbook=                               | yes      | 3             | =figure= =table= =footnote= (printed without the chapter) |
| memoir     | =memoir=                                | yes      | 1             | =equation= =figure= =table= =footnote=     |
| article    | every other class, and no class         | no       | 3             | —                                          |

In book and report, =\theequation=, =\thefigure= and =\thetable= leave
the chapter out while it is 0 (=Figure 1= before the first chapter).

** Sections

| Level | Command          | Numbered when                    | Printed                                    |
|-------+------------------+----------------------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| −1    | =\part=          | =secnumdepth= ≥ −1               | I, II, … (resets nothing)                  |
| 0     | =\chapter=       | in the main matter               | 1, 2, …; A, B, … after =\appendix=         |
| 1     | =\section=       | =secnumdepth= ≥ 1                | 1 (article), 1.1 (chaptered); A in an article's appendix |
| 2     | =\subsection=    | =secnumdepth= ≥ 2                | 1.1, 1.1.1                                 |
| 3     | =\subsubsection= | =secnumdepth= ≥ 3                | parent's number, a dot, its own            |
| 4, 5  | =\paragraph=, =\subparagraph= | =secnumdepth= ≥ 4, 5 | the same                              |

A starred sectioning command is not numbered and does not step its
counter. Each level resets the levels below it; resets cascade (a new
chapter resets =section=, which resets =subsection=, and so on).
=\appendix= resets =section= and =subsection= (article) or =chapter= and
=section= (chaptered) and switches to letters. =\frontmatter= and
=\backmatter= stop numbering chapters; =\mainmatter= resumes it.

** Counters

=\setcounter{c}{n}= and =\addtocounter{c}{n}= set any counter.
=\numberwithin{c}{w}= (amsmath) and =\counterwithin{c}{w}= reset =c= with
=w= and print it as =w='s number, a dot, its own; =\counterwithout=
undoes it; =\newcounter{c}[w]= resets =c= with =w= but prints it alone.
=\refstepcounter{c}= steps =c= and makes it what =\label= points at.
=\renewcommand{\thec}{…}= changes how =c= prints, through =\arabic=,
=\roman=, =\Roman=, =\alph=, =\Alph= and other =\the…= commands.

** Lists

=enumerate= numbers its items 1., 2., …; nested, (a), i., A. (the standard
classes' four levels); a =\label= on an item points at =2=, =2a=, =2(b)i=,
=2(b)iA=. =\item[x]= shows =x= and numbers nothing. =itemize= and
=description= number nothing. enumitem's =label== option changes the
printed label and what =\ref= prints.

** Floats and captions

A =\caption= in =figure= (=figure*=, =wrapfigure=) steps =figure=; in
=table= (=table*=, =wraptable=, =longtable=) it steps =table=. In
=subfigure= and =subtable= (subcaption) and for =\subfloat[caption]{…}=
(subfig) the caption is lettered a, b, … within its float, and =\ref=
prints the float's number and the letter (=1a=). =\captionof{figure}{…}=
steps =figure= outside a float. A caption elsewhere is not numbered.

** Theorems

=\newtheorem{env}{Title}= declares an environment with its own counter;
=\newtheorem{env}[other]{Title}= shares =other='s;
=\newtheorem{env}{Title}[within]= numbers within =within= (=1.1=);
=\newtheorem*= declares an unnumbered one. thmtools' =\declaretheorem=
does the same with keys (=name=, =numberwithin=, =sibling=,
=numbered=no=). The environment's optional argument is its note.
=proof= (amsthm) is unnumbered and ends with ∎.

Journals' classes declare theorems themselves (llncs, svjour3, acmart,
SIAM's), and papers use theorems declared in files a reader may not have.
A reader SHOULD take an undeclared environment of a usual name as that
theorem on its own counter: =theorem=, =lemma=, =corollary=,
=proposition=, =definition=, =remark=, =example=, =conjecture=, =claim=,
=assumption= and the like, their usual short forms (=thm=, =lem=, =lma=,
=cor=, =prop=, =defn=, =rmk=, =expl=, =assump=, …), and those after =my=,
=custom= or =new= (=myassump=).

** Equations

| Environment                                                           | Numbers                                                       |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| =equation=, =multline=                                                | one                                                           |
| =align=, =gather=, =flalign=, =alignat=, =eqnarray=                   | one per line; each =\\= ends a line, and so does the environment's end |
| the starred forms, =\[…\]=, =displaymath=                             | none; =\tag{x}= numbers a line =(x)= (not in =$$…$$=)          |
| =subequations=                                                        | steps =equation= once; lines inside are =3a=, =3b=, …         |
| =split=, =aligned=, =gathered=, =cases=, the matrices                 | nothing of their own (inner)                                  |

=\nonumber= and =\notag= take a line's number away; =\tag{x}= replaces it.

** Labels and references

=\label{k}= takes the number of the last thing numbered in its scope by
=\refstepcounter= (a section, an equation line, a caption, a theorem, an
enumerated item, a footnote). An environment and a footnote's text are
groups: at their end what =\label= points at is restored. A label with
nothing numbered before it points at nothing.

| Command                    | Prints for a label                                   |
|----------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| =\ref{k}=                  | its number                                           |
| =\eqref{k}=                | its number in parentheses                            |
| =\pageref{k}=              | its page                                             |
| =\autoref{k}= (hyperref)   | the kind's name and the number: Section 2, Figure 3  |
| =\cref{k}=, =\Cref{k}= (cleveref) | the same, lower- and upper-case; =\cref{a,b}= lists them |
| =\nameref{k}=              | the title of what it points at                       |

When two labels have the same key, pdfLaTeX writes both to the =.aux=
file and the last one written is the one =\ref= reads on the next run;
a reader SHOULD report the clash.

** Citations and the bibliography

=\cite[post]{k1,k2}= and its variants (natbib's =\citet= =\citep=
=\citeauthor= =\citeyear= =\citealt= =\citealp= with optional pre- and
post-notes =[pre][post]=; biblatex's =\parencite= =\textcite=
=\autocite= =\footcite= and their capitalized forms) cite entries of the
=.bib= files named by =\bibliography{a,b}= or =\addbibresource{a.bib}=,
or of a =thebibliography= environment's =\bibitem[label]{key}=. The
label printed follows =\bibliographystyle= (numbers or author–year).

* Mathematics

** Formulas

| Delimiters                     | Formula                   |
|--------------------------------+---------------------------|
| =$…$=, =\(…\)=, =\begin{math}= | inline                    |
| =$$…$$=, =\[…\]=, =\begin{displaymath}= | displayed, unnumbered |
| the environments of §9.7       | displayed                 |

A formula ends at its closing delimiter; one not closed by the end of
its paragraph is an error. Inside a formula, =\text{…}=, =\mbox{…}=,
=\textrm{…}= and the other text commands are text again, and =$= inside
them opens a formula. =\ensuremath{x}= is math in text and in math.

** What a formula renderer must read

A renderer that draws formulas MUST accept the commands of LaTeX's math
mode and of =amsmath= and =amssymb=, and SHOULD accept, as their usual
definitions: =\mathds= =\mathbbm= (as =\mathbb=), =\bm= =\boldsymbol=,
=\nicefrac= =\sfrac=, =\tfrac= =\dfrac= =\cfrac=, =\xrightarrow=
=\xleftarrow=, =\operatorname=, =\DeclareMathOperator='s operators,
=\text=, =\intertext=, =\tag=, =\substack=, the =cases= =dcases= =matrix=
families, =\overset= =\underset= =\stackrel=, =\overbrace= =\underbrace=,
the physics package's delimiters and Dirac notation (=\abs= =\norm=
=\qty= =\ket= =\bra=), and the
letter families papers define in loops (=\cA= … =\cZ= as =\mathcal=,
=\bA= … as =\mathbb=, =\sA= … as =\mathscr=, =\fA= … as =\mathfrak=)
when the document does not define them itself.

The document's own macros (§8.1) apply in formulas: a renderer MUST use
the definitions that read, in their order, and MUST NOT let one
definition it cannot read stop the formulas after it. A definition is
read when it, a formula after it and its use draw nothing but the
formula: a definition that leaves text behind (an optional argument's
default the renderer does not take, =\newcommand{\x}[2][]{#2}=, read as
two arguments and a stray =]#2=) MUST be taken as =\def\x#1{#1}=, the
default put in the first argument's place.

Commutative diagrams (=tikz-cd=, =xymatrix=), =\buildrel=, plain TeX's
=\matrix= and =\over=, the size environments and =\textcolor= with a
model are written in formulas often enough that a renderer SHOULD read
them or have TeX typeset the formula.

* Error recovery

Documents as found are not all well formed: braces left open, an
environment not closed, a formula running past its paragraph. TeX stops
at the first error; a reader MUST NOT. It MUST:

- keep every byte of the source: what it does not understand is text;
- close what is open at the end of its container (a group at the end of
  its environment's body, a formula at the end of its paragraph, an
  environment at the end of the document), and report it;
- pair =\end{x}= with the nearest open =x=, as TeX does;
- read a construct outside §4 to §10 as its source: an unknown command as
  its name and the groups after it, an unknown environment as its
  delimiters and its body.

A renderer SHOULD show such a construct as its source rather than guess
its meaning; when TeX is at hand it MAY have TeX typeset it (a formula
the renderer cannot read, a picture) and show the result.

* Testing a reader

** Coverage

Take the corpus (=tools/arxiv-sample.py= fetches it; the list of papers is
fixed by the year and the share per category) and measure, for each
field, the bytes of the body the reader leaves as source. =kalem
latex-coverage DIR= does it for Kalem and reports the constructs that
leave the most. A reader that follows this appendix leaves less than
0.1% of the body in each field; Kalem's target is the same (Part IV,
decision D30).

** Typesetting

=tools/latex-typeset-fuzz.py= writes random lines of text (words,
punctuation, dashes, accents, symbols, spaces, fonts, case changes,
colors, boxes, verbatim) and has pdfLaTeX put each in a box; it writes
the reader's text of the same lines in a box too and compares the glyphs.
=--each= runs every construct alone. Every rule of §4 and §5 is checked
so.

** Numbering

=tools/latex-numbering-fuzz.py= writes random documents of sections,
equations, floats, theorems, lists, footnotes, counters and labels,
compiles them, and compares the =.aux= file's numbers with the reader's.
Every rule of §9 is checked so.

** Parsing

A reader MUST give, for any input, a structure whose leaves are the
whole source in order. An incremental reader MUST give, after an edit,
the structure a full read gives (Kalem's fuzz target =latex_reparse=
checks it).
