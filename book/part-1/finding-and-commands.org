Ctrl+F finds, Ctrl+H finds and replaces. Every match is marked; Enter
and Shift+Enter step through them. In the replace field, Enter
replaces one match and Alt+Enter all of them, in one undo step. Alt+R,
or the =.*= button, makes the search a regular expression, with =$1=
for its groups in the replacement.

Ctrl+Shift+P (or F1; Ctrl+G in a terminal) opens the command palette:
every command that applies, by its title, with its category and keys.
Type part of a title, or a command's ID (=org.todo.cycle=, or =org
todo cycle=; =kalem commands= lists them). Commands that need
something, such as a heading level, ask for it. What you type there,
as in any question Kalem asks, is edited as a line: the arrows, Home,
End, Backspace and Delete, by words with Ctrl (Option on macOS).

/Search Lines/ (=SPC s b= with Vim keys, and =SPC s s=) lists the lines
of the document that hold every word typed, in any order, and the
cursor follows the list: Enter stays there, Escape goes back.
Lower-case words match either case; a word with a capital matches
exactly. =SPC s B= searches every open document, =SPC s i= lists the
headings (=SPC s I= of every open document), =SPC s S= starts with the
word at the cursor, =SPC s d= searches the files under the document's
folder, =SPC s D= another folder, =SPC s P= another project, =SPC s o=
looks the word or the selection up online (=search.online_url= says
where), and =SPC *= searches the project for the word at the cursor or
the selection.

Bookmarks are named places in files: /Set Bookmark/ (=SPC b m=) names
the cursor's place (the file and line when you give no name), /Jump to
Bookmark/ (=SPC RET= or =SPC s m=) lists them and opens one at its
place, and /Delete Bookmark/ (=SPC b M=) takes one away. They are kept
in =bookmarks.json= in Kalem's state folder, for every project, with
the text of their line, so a bookmark follows its line when lines are
added above it.

The menus hold the commands of the document you are in: a LaTeX file
has LaTeX's, a CSV file a Table menu. On macOS they are in the menu
bar at the top of the screen; on Linux and Windows the window has its
own menu bar above the toolbar (=ui.menu_bar = false= hides it). F10
lists every menu item in the palette, titled with its menu (/Edit ›
Select All/), in both editors; it is how the terminal editor, which
has no menu bar, reaches them. Where this book names a menu item, F10
and its name find it.
