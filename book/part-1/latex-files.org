=.tex= files open rendered, as the document reads, and stay standard
LaTeX byte for byte: nothing is rewritten. Sectioning commands show as
headings with the numbers LaTeX gives them, =\emph=, =\textbf=,
=\texttt= and their kin show their text styled with the command and its
braces hidden until the cursor reaches them, quotes and dashes (=``=,
=''=, =--=, =---=), =~=, =\&=, =\%= and =\\= show as the characters they
typeset, =\maketitle= shows the title, the authors and the date, and
=center=, =flushleft= and =flushright= align their lines. In =itemize=,
=enumerate= and =description= lists each =\item= shows its bullet, its
number in LaTeX's style for its depth (=1.=, =(a)=, =i.=, =A.=, or what
enumitem's =label= and =start= say) or its own =[label]=, indented by
its depth. Formulas (=$…$=, =\(…\)=, =\[…\]=, =$$…$$= and the math
environments) are typeset away from the cursor, equations with the
numbers LaTeX gives them, using the macros the document defines; a math
environment on lines of its own shows as one formula until the cursor
enters it. In =figure= and =table=, =\includegraphics= shows the
picture (PNG, JPEG or SVG, found in the document's folder or a
=\graphicspath= folder with the extensions LaTeX tries) at the width
=width== asks for, =\centering= centers it, and =\caption= shows as
"Figure 1: …" with LaTeX's number (=Şekil= and =Tablo= with babel's
=turkish=). A PDF or EPS picture shows as its name. =\ref=, =\eqref=,
=\cref=, =\autoref= and =\nameref= show the numbers (or titles) they
resolve to, in red as =??= when no =\label= has the key; citations show
the authors and years of the entries in the =\bibliography= or
=\addbibresource= files, with their notes, and the entry cited shows
in the status bar at the cursor and in a tooltip under the mouse, as
the bibliography of a CSL style has it (the style closest to biblatex's
=style== or to =\bibliographystyle=: =ieee= for =plain= and =unsrt=,
=apa= for =apalike= and natbib's, else =chicago-author-date=). BibTeX
entries pasted into the document go into its first bibliography file
(keys it has already are left) and a =\cite= of them is pasted instead.
A =.bib= file opens as a grid: each entry one row with its key, type,
authors, title and year, and the entry at the cursor as its source.
The BibTeX menu (the palette in the terminal) sorts the rows by author,
year, title or key without changing the file (/File Order/ goes back),
adds an entry (/New Entry/) and sets a field of the entry at the cursor
(/Set Field/; an empty value removes the field), writing only what
changes. The status bar counts the entries.
=\footnote= shows its raised number before its dimmed text, and =\url=
and =\href= show as links. /Open Link/ (Ctrl-click, Cmd-click on macOS)
follows the command at the cursor: a reference to its =\label=, =\url=
and =\href= to the browser, =\input=, =\include= and =\subfile= to their
file, =\includegraphics= to its picture and a citation to the
bibliography file. Theorems declared with =\newtheorem= show
their names and numbers ("Theorem 1.1 (Pythagoras).") and =proof= its
"Proof." and ∎; =\paragraph= is a bold heading in the text. =quote=,
=quotation=, =verse= and =abstract= are indented, the abstract under
its centered title, and =\verb= and =\lstinline= show their code in
monospace, =\lstinline= in the colors of its language (its
=language== option or =\lstset='s). The front matter of the common
journal classes shows as the class prints it: IEEEtran's author blocks,
"Index Terms" and =\IEEEPARstart=, acmart's affiliations, e-mail and
keywords, elsarticle's =frontmatter= and =keyword=, llncs's
=\institute=, RevTeX's =\affiliation= and =\pacs=. A
=tabular= (also =tabularx=, =array=, =longtable=) written one row a
line, with plain columns (=l=, =c=, =r=, =p{}=, =|=), shows as a grid
with its rules (=\hline=, =\toprule=, =\midrule=, =\bottomrule=) until
the cursor enters it; there Tab and Shift+Tab go from cell to cell.
A =\multicolumn= is one cell across the columns it covers, and a rule
written after a row's =\\= is drawn under the row.
Other tables stay as source. The code of
=verbatim=, =lstlisting= and =minted= is monospace, colored by the
language its options name (graphical editor). Comments, =comment=
environments and the text between =\iffalse= and its =\fi= are
dimmed, and fold to their first line away from the cursor; commands Kalem does not render stay as they are written. The
source view (Ctrl+/) shows the text itself. The preamble shows only
its first line until the cursor goes into it. So does an environment of eight
lines or more that Kalem shows as source (a =tikzpicture=, say). Documents of several files
are numbered as one: the root document comes from a =% !TEX root= line,
the main file of a subfile, a =NAME.tex.latexmain= file, or the document
up the folders that includes the file; a file of the project shows the
numbers it has in the whole document, and its references to labels in
the other files resolve (the other files are read from the disk, again
when they change). The outline panel
(Ctrl+Shift+O) lists the document's sections with their numbers. A
=\tableofcontents= line shows the table of contents LaTeX will print
there, each entry leading to its section. The =\end{document}= line lists the
document's footnotes, each leading to its =\footnote=.

What =kalem check= reports shows in the text a moment after typing
stops: a red wavy line under problems (a group or environment left open,
a label defined twice, a reference or citation key nothing defines, a
file or picture not found) and a blue one under matters of style
(=\bf= and its kin, =$$=, a space before =\ref= where =~= belongs,
=...=, ="=). With the cursor on one the status bar says what it is, and
so does a tooltip under the mouse (graphical editor). /Quick Fix/
(Ctrl+., Alt+. in a terminal) makes the obvious change where there is
one: =\bfseries= for =\bf=, =~= for the space, =\ldots{}= for =...=,
=\[…\]= for =$$…$$=; it takes the problem at the cursor, or the first
on its line. The errors and warnings of the last /Build PDF/ are flagged
on their lines too. /Next Problem/ and /Previous Problem/ (Alt+F8,
Alt+Shift+F8) go from one flagged place to the next and say what it is. /Show Problems/ lists them all with their lines;
choosing one goes to it.

Typing ="= in the text writes LaTeX's quotes, =``= to open and =''= to
close; typed again right away, it is a plain ="= (not in documents whose
language makes ="= a shorthand, such as German).
Enter on a list item starts the next =\item= (on an empty one, it leaves
the list); Tab and Shift+Tab nest an item under the one before it and
take it out again. Typing =\begin{name}= adds =\end{name}=, and renaming
one end of an environment renames the other. Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+U and
Ctrl+Shift+K put =\textbf=, =\emph=, =\underline= and =\texttt= around
the selection or the word, or take them off. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6 make the
line a heading of that level with the class's commands (=\chapter= first
in books and reports, =\section= in articles), Ctrl+0 makes it text
again, and Alt+Shift+Left and Right promote and demote a section with
its subsections, Alt+Shift+Up and Down move it past its neighbor. Tab on
a section's line folds it as Tab folds an Org heading: its text and
subsections hidden, then its subsections shown, then everything.

In math, =$= pairs (and steps over the closing =$=), =\(= and =\[= get
their closing pair and =\left(= its =\right)=, and Tab goes to the next
empty ={}= (after =\frac=, =\sqrt=, and the limits =\sum=, =\int= and
their kin complete with); in a matrix or =cases=, which complete as two
rows of two cells, Tab goes to the next cell. While the cursor is in a formula,
it shows rendered under the cursor. /Display Math/ turns =$…$= into
=\[…\]= and back, and /Number Equation/ adds or takes off the star of the
environment at the cursor. /Insert Figure/, /Insert Table/ (with
booktabs' rules when the document loads it), /Insert Equation/ and
/Insert Citation/ (the entries of the bibliography to choose from) write
the usual constructs with a caption and a label to fill. /Insert Figure/
asks in turn for the picture, its width (a share of the line, or a
length) and its caption. Command completion knows the commands and
environments of the packages the preamble loads, with their arguments.

/New from Template…/ (File menu) writes a new document beside the current
one from a template and opens it: an article, a report, a book, a beamer
presentation, a letter, a CV, a thesis, or a Turkish thesis laid out as
YÖK's guidelines ask (which needs babel's Turkish, as TeX Live's full
installation has). The templates themselves never change.

With pandoc installed, /Export as HTML/, /Markdown/ and /Word/ write the
project's root document in those formats for co-authors who do not use
LaTeX, and /Convert to Org/ writes an Org file beside it: one way, the
LaTeX file stays as it is.

Completion (as you type, or Alt+/) offers command names after =\= (the
document's own macros too), environments after =\begin{= (with their
=\end=) and =\end{=, the keys of the bibliography after =\cite{= and its
kin (matched on key, authors and title), the labels after =\ref{= and its
kin with what they number, files after =\input{= and
=\includegraphics{=, and packages after =\usepackage{=.

/Build PDF/ (F5) builds the project's root document in the background
with =latexmk= (or the engine alone, with =biber= or =bibtex= between
its runs): pdfLaTeX, or the engine a =% !TEX program = xelatex= line or
the =latex.engine= setting names, or XeLaTeX for documents that load
=fontspec=. The status bar gives the first error at its file and line,
or the PDF and the number of warnings; a missing package comes with the
command that installs it; the problems are flagged in the text, and
/Cancel Build/ stops a build that runs. =latex.output_directory= sends the output to
a folder, and =latex.build_on_save= builds each time the document is
saved. On the command line, =kalem latex build FILE= does the same
(=--format json= for the problems as data).
