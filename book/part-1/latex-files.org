=.tex= files open rendered, as the document reads, and stay standard
LaTeX byte for byte: nothing is rewritten, and what Kalem does not
render stays as it is written. The source view (Ctrl+/) shows the text
itself.

* What is rendered

- Structure :: sectioning commands show as headings with the numbers
  LaTeX gives them, =\paragraph= as a bold heading in the text,
  =\maketitle= as the title, authors and date, and the front matter of
  IEEEtran, acmart, elsarticle, llncs and RevTeX as each class prints
  it. The preamble shows only its first line until the cursor enters
  it, and so does a long environment shown as source (a =tikzpicture=,
  say).
- Text :: =\emph=, =\textbf=, =\texttt= and their kin show their text
  styled, with the command and braces hidden until the cursor reaches
  them; =``=, =''=, =--=, =---=, =~=, =\&=, =\%= and =\\= show as the
  characters they typeset; =center=, =flushleft=, =flushright=,
  =quote=, =quotation=, =verse= and =abstract= are aligned or indented
  as LaTeX prints them. =\footnote= shows its raised number before its
  dimmed text, =\url= and =\href= are links, and comments, =comment=
  environments and =\iffalse= … =\fi= are dimmed and fold.
- Lists :: in =itemize=, =enumerate= and =description= each =\item=
  shows its bullet, its number in LaTeX's style for its depth (=1.=,
  =(a)=, =i.=, or what enumitem's =label= and =start= say) or its own
  =[label]=.
- Formulas :: =$…$=, =\(…\)=, =\[…\]=, =$$…$$= and the math
  environments are typeset away from the cursor, equations with their
  numbers, using the document's macros. Theorems declared with
  =\newtheorem= show their names and numbers, and =proof= its "Proof."
  and ∎.
- Figures and tables :: =\includegraphics= shows the picture (PNG,
  JPEG or SVG, found as LaTeX finds it) at the width asked for, and
  =\caption= shows as "Figure 1: …" with LaTeX's number (=Şekil= and
  =Tablo= with babel's =turkish=). A =tabular= (also =tabularx=,
  =array=, =longtable=) written one row a line, with plain columns,
  shows as a grid with its rules until the cursor enters it, where
  Tab and Shift+Tab go from cell to cell; other tables stay as source.
- Code :: =verbatim=, =lstlisting=, =minted=, =\verb= and =\lstinline=
  are monospace, colored by the language their options name.
- References and citations :: =\ref=, =\eqref=, =\cref=, =\autoref= and
  =\nameref= show the numbers or titles they resolve to, in red as =??=
  when no =\label= has the key. Citations show the authors and years of
  the entries in the =\bibliography= or =\addbibresource= files, and
  the entry cited shows in the status bar and in a tooltip, formatted
  by the CSL style closest to the document's bibliography style.
  BibTeX entries pasted into the document go into its first
  bibliography file, and a =\cite= of them is pasted instead.

/Open Link/ (Ctrl-click, Cmd-click on macOS) follows the command at
the cursor: a reference to its =\label=, =\url= to the browser,
=\input=, =\include= and =\subfile= to their file, =\includegraphics=
to its picture, a citation to the bibliography file.

Documents of several files are numbered as one: the root is a =% !TEX
root= line, the main file of a subfile, a =NAME.tex.latexmain= file,
or the document up the folders that includes the file; each file
shows the numbers it has in the whole document, and references to
labels in the other files resolve. The outline panel (Ctrl+Shift+O)
lists the sections with their numbers, a =\tableofcontents= line
shows the table of contents LaTeX will print there, and the
=\end{document}= line lists the document's footnotes.

* Problems

What =kalem check= reports shows in the text a moment after typing
stops: a red wavy line under problems (a group or environment left
open, a label defined twice, an undefined reference or citation key, a
missing file or picture) and a blue one under matters of style (=\bf=
and its kin, =$$=, a space before =\ref= where =~= belongs, =...=,
="=). The status bar, and a tooltip, say what it is. /Quick Fix/
(Ctrl+., Alt+. in a terminal) makes the obvious change: =\bfseries=
for =\bf=, =~= for the space, =\ldots{}= for =...=, =\[…\]= for
=$$…$$=. The errors and warnings of the last /Build PDF/ are flagged
on their lines too; /Next Problem/ and /Previous Problem/ (Alt+F8,
Alt+Shift+F8) go from one to the next, and /Show Problems/ lists them
all.

* Typing

- ="= writes LaTeX's quotes, =``= to open and =''= to close; typed
  again right away, it is a plain ="= (not in languages where ="= is a
  shorthand, such as German).
- Enter on a list item starts the next =\item=, and on an empty one
  leaves the list; Tab and Shift+Tab nest an item and take it out.
- =\begin{name}= adds its =\end{name}=, and renaming one end of an
  environment renames the other.
- Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+U and Ctrl+Shift+K put =\textbf=, =\emph=,
  =\underline= and =\texttt= around the selection or the word, or take
  them off.
- Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6 make the line a heading of that level with the
  class's commands (=\chapter= first in books and reports, =\section=
  in articles), Ctrl+0 makes it text again, Alt+Shift+Left and Right
  promote and demote a section with its subsections, Alt+Shift+Up and
  Down move it past its neighbor, and Tab on a section's line folds it.
- In math, =$= pairs, =\(=, =\[= and =\left(= get their closing pair,
  and Tab goes to the next empty ={}= or, in a matrix or =cases=, to
  the next cell; the formula shows rendered under the cursor as you
  type. /Display Math/ turns =$…$= into =\[…\]= and back, and /Number
  Equation/ adds or takes off the star of the environment at the
  cursor.
- /Insert Figure/, /Insert Table/ (with booktabs' rules when the
  document loads it), /Insert Equation/ and /Insert Citation/ write
  the usual constructs with a caption and a label to fill.

Completion (as you type, or Ctrl+Space, Alt+/) offers commands after
=\=, the document's macros and the commands of the packages the
preamble loads included, environments after =\begin{= and =\end{=, the
bibliography's keys after =\cite{= and its kin, labels after =\ref{=
and its kin with what they number, files after =\input{= and
=\includegraphics{=, and packages after =\usepackage{=.

* Templates, export and building

/New from Template…/ (File menu) writes a new document beside the
current one from a template and opens it: an article, a report, a
book, a beamer presentation, a letter, a CV, a thesis, or a Turkish
thesis laid out as YÖK's guidelines ask. With pandoc installed,
/Export as HTML/, /Markdown/ and /Word/ write the project's root
document in those formats, and /Convert to Org/ writes an Org file
beside it; the LaTeX file stays as it is.

/Build PDF/ (F5) builds the root document in the background with
=latexmk=, or the engine alone with =biber= or =bibtex= between its
runs: pdfLaTeX, the engine a =% !TEX program = xelatex= line or the
=latex.engine= setting names, or XeLaTeX for documents that load
=fontspec=. The status bar gives the first error at its file and line,
or the PDF and the number of warnings; a missing package comes with
the command that installs it; /Cancel Build/ stops a build.
=latex.output_directory= sends the output to a folder,
=latex.build_on_save= builds on each save, and =kalem latex build
FILE= does the same from the command line (=--format json= for the
problems as data).

Builds write a SyncTeX file beside the PDF, which says where each
source line is typeset. /Show in PDF/ opens the PDF at the page of the
cursor's line, in the files a root document includes too; in the PDF,
Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on macOS) on a line of text opens the source file
at the line it came from. A PDF built before has no SyncTeX file:
build it again.

Coming from Emacs, [[file:latex-for-auctex-users.org][LaTeX for AUCTeX users]] maps AUCTeX's and RefTeX's commands
to Kalem's.

* Beside Overleaf and co-authors

Kalem works on the files of a project, so a project shared through Git
is edited as it is: an Overleaf project cloned with Overleaf's Git
integration (=git clone https://git.overleaf.com/…=, on the plans that
include it) or a repository on GitHub that co-authors use, edited in
Kalem, built on this computer, and pushed back.

- Only what you change changes. Kalem keeps every byte it was not asked
  to change (line endings, spaces, comments, the order of the preamble),
  so a co-author's diff shows your edits and nothing else.
- Files that change on disk while open (after =git pull=, or a
  co-author's edit synced) are read again; a file you have changed too
  asks which version to keep.
- The root document: Overleaf's "main document" setting is not in the
  files, so Kalem finds the root from them ([[file:../part-2/latex.org::*The root document][The root document]]);
  a =% !TEX root = main.tex= line in a chapter file names it for every
  editor.
- The engine: Overleaf builds with pdfLaTeX unless the project's menu
  says otherwise. A =% !TEX program = xelatex= line, or the setting
  =latex.engine=, makes Kalem's build use the same engine, so the PDF
  matches the one co-authors see.
- Build outputs: /Ignore Build Outputs in Git/ adds what LaTeX writes
  beside the document (=*.aux=, =*.log=, =*.synctex.gz=, …, the root's
  PDF and =latex.output_directory=) to the repository's =.gitignore=, so
  they are not pushed; a build in a repository without them offers it.

* BibTeX files

A =.bib= file opens as a grid: each entry one row with its key, type,
authors, title and year, and the entry at the cursor as its source.
The BibTeX menu (the palette in the terminal) sorts the rows by
author, year, title or key without changing the file (/File Order/
goes back), adds an entry (/New Entry/) and sets a field of the entry
at the cursor (/Set Field/; an empty value removes it), writing only
what changes.
