This part is the specification of the Kalem format, =.klm=: Kalem's own
text document format, designed from scratch rather than as an extension
of Org. Its chapters are numbered as the sections of RFC 0003 they come
from (=rfcs/0003-kalem-format.md=), so "(14.4)" is rule 4 of chapter 14.

* Where it stands

- *Version:* draft 0.2, the working specification accepted by the owner
  on 2026-09-30, with the clarifications the parser spike led to (RFC
  0003, appendix B). The format's own version is independent of
  Kalem's; the changes are in the [[file:changelog.org][changelog of the format]].
- *Implemented:* the parser =klm-syntax= (task T2.13.3, grown from the
  spike of T2.13.2): every node with its byte range, recovery from
  ill-formed input exactly as chapter 15 says, a reparse from the
  enclosing top-level block after an edit that gives the same tree as a
  full parse, and the canonical form of chapter 14. It reads every
  example of this part and the three samples of =tests/klm-spec/samples/=,
  never panics on thousands of mangled inputs, and parses a megabyte in
  about 50 ms. Kalem's core has the format on its mode contract (the
  outline, Format Document, diagnostics, HTML). =kalem check= lists what
  the parser recovered from; =kalem fmt= writes the canonical form of a
  well-formed file, and with =--repair= of an ill-formed one, showing
  the change; the editors save a well-formed =.klm= in its canonical
  form, and /Repair Document/ fixes an ill-formed one. A line per sentence
  (=\meta[format=sentence]=, chapter 14) is written when the document
  asks for it. The editors do not yet render or edit =.klm= files as
  this format (T2.13.5, T2.13.7).
- *Open questions* are listed in section 21 of RFC 0003; the survey of
  other formats (section 2), the alternatives considered (section 20)
  and the record of the experiments (appendices A and B) stay there. The
  RFC is the design record; this part is the specification.

* How it is written

- A syntax change goes through this part, its changelog and the
  conformance suite =tests/klm-spec/=, never through code alone.
- Every example written in the format (a =klm= source block) is a file
  of the suite, with its expected model as JSON, its canonical form and
  its HTML; =kalem book check= fails on an example that is not.
- The words /must/, /should/ and /may/ carry the meaning of RFC 2119.
