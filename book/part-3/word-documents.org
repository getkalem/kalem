The =docx= plugin of =getkalem/plugins= opens Word documents as
themselves: the file is a package of WordprocessingML parts, and the
plugin reads, edits and writes those parts, never a conversion of
them. Kalem lays the paragraphs out itself, through the plugin API's
=flow= interface, and reviews the comments and tracked changes through
its =annotations= interface (see [[file:plugins.org][Plugins]], "Viewers of flowing text").
The plugin is installed from the plugin list; its README and its work
list (=plugins/docx/docx_todo.md=) are kept with it.

* The standard and the oracle

- *The standard* is ECMA-376 part 1, WordprocessingML, in its
  transitional namespace (the strict one is read as it), with the
  =w14=, =w15= and =w16= additions Word writes read and kept.
- *The oracle* is Word 2016 and later (Microsoft 365): "as Word writes
  it" is how each edit is written, element order following the
  schema's sequences, which Word checks. Word cannot be driven from a
  script past its file access prompt, so it is checked by hand.
- *LibreOffice Writer* 7.3 is the second oracle, by hand too: =soffice
  --convert-to txt= and =--convert-to docx= open the edited files,
  show the edits and keep the tracked insertions and deletions with
  their authors.
- *The corpus* has four files from three producers: python-docx over
  its default template, a package written part by part with every
  construct the plugin reads, and each of the two saved again by
  LibreOffice. The tests check that each saves back byte for byte, and
  what each edit changes.

* Which files

=.docx=, =.docm=, =.dotx= and =.dotm=. A file with one of these
extensions that starts as a ZIP archive is the plugin's at once; one
that does not is tried as a document encrypted with a password to
open, which Word writes as a compound file. A file of another
extension is not the plugin's whatever it holds.

* What is read

- *The package* through its relationships, never by a part's name: the
  body, the headers and footers, the footnotes and endnotes, the
  comments, the styles, the numbering, the settings and the theme.
  Every element is read with its byte span; an element the plugin does
  not know is kept with its span and shown by name.
- *Styles* as 17.7.2 puts them together: the document defaults, the
  table style and its conditional formats as =w:tblLook= turns them
  on, the numbering level, the paragraph style and its =basedOn=
  chain, the character style, then direct formatting; the toggle
  properties combined across style kinds as 17.7.3 says. Theme fonts
  and colors are resolved, a tint or shade computed as Word computes
  it.
- *Lists* counted as Word counts them: instances of one abstract
  definition counting together, =w:startOverride=, =w:lvlRestart=,
  legal numbering, levels linked to styles; labels in every common
  number format, bullets in Symbol and Wingdings as the Unicode
  characters they draw.
- *Fields* show their results, simple and complex, nested;
  =HYPERLINK= fields and =w:hyperlink= are links. Notes are numbered
  in order of reference as =w:footnotePr= and =w:endnotePr= say.
- *Comments* with their ranges, and *tracked changes*: insertions,
  deletions, moves, formatting changes of runs and paragraphs, and
  paragraph marks inserted or deleted, each with its author and date.
- *A password to open* ([MS-OFFCRYPTO]): agile encryption, Word
  2010's and later's, is read and written, and Word 2007's standard
  encryption is read. Kalem asks for the password, hidden as typed.

* What is shown

Word's web layout, without pages: a document of the editor whose lines
are the paragraphs, in both editors.

- Each paragraph is a line, drawn with its runs' look: bold, italic,
  underline, strike, caps and small caps, raised and lowered text,
  color, highlight and typeface; a size in proportion to the body
  text's, so a heading is larger than the text around it. A list item
  has its label before it; a heading is in the outline.
- A tab, a line break, a page break and a column break are one
  character each in the paragraph's line; a picture, a note's mark and
  an object are one object character.
- A table row whose cells hold one paragraph each is one line, its
  cells apart by tabs and the line marked =▏= at its start. A row with
  more in a cell shows a paragraph a line, the row's first marked =▏=
  and each further cell's first =▏┆=. A nested table's paragraphs are
  shown in the cell that holds it.
- The first section's header comes before the body, marked =Header ▏=,
  and its footer after it, marked =Footer ▏=. Footnotes and endnotes
  follow, each marked with its number. A text box follows the
  paragraph it is anchored in, marked =▕=.
- Charts, shapes, SmartArt, equations and embedded objects are shown
  by name, as placeholders.
- Text a comment is on is highlighted; inserted text is underlined in
  green and deleted text struck through in red. The status bar says
  the comment at the cursor with its author, or who made the change
  there.

* What is edited

Typing, Enter, Backspace and Delete edit the document as typing in Word
does, in the body, a table cell, the header, the footer and the notes:

- Typed text goes into the run before it and takes its look; over a
  selection it takes the look of the selection's first character. A
  deletion shortens, empties or removes the runs it covers.
- Enter splits the paragraph, the elements open where it splits with
  it; after a heading the new paragraph takes the style's next style.
  Backspace at a paragraph's start joins it to the one before; a
  deletion across paragraphs is one edit.
- Each of Kalem's edits is one step of the document's own history:
  Undo and Redo go through it, and undoing everything saves the file as
  it was opened, byte for byte.
- In a document that tracks changes, typed text is written as an
  insertion, deleted text kept as a deletion, and Enter and Backspace
  mark the paragraph mark inserted or deleted, each with the author,
  the date and an ID of its own. The author is the setting
  =user.name=, else the system account's name.

The commands of the /Review/ category act on the annotations:

| Command              | What it does                                                       |
|----------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Next Change, Previous Change | Moves to the next or the previous tracked change           |
| Accept Change        | Keeps the change at the cursor: an insertion's text stays, a deletion's goes, a deleted paragraph mark joins its paragraphs (in the body only), a formatting change keeps the new look |
| Reject Change        | Undoes it: an insertion's text goes, a deletion's text comes back, a formatting change restores the old look |
| Accept All Changes, Reject All Changes | The same for every change, in every part of the document |
| Track Changes        | Turns tracking on or off (=w:trackRevisions= in the settings)       |
| Next Comment, Previous Comment | Moves to the next or the previous comment                |
| New Comment (Ctrl+Alt+M) | Puts a comment on the selected text, in the body or a note: its range's start and end around the text, a run split where the range starts or ends inside it, a run with its reference after the end |
| Reply to Comment     | Answers the comment at the cursor: a comment anchored on the same text and named as its answer; an answer to an answer answers the first comment, as in Word |
| Edit Comment         | Changes the text of the comment at the cursor, a paragraph a line; the palette starts with its text. Its author, date and mark stay, and its last paragraph keeps its ID, so its answers and its done mark stay its own |
| Resolve Comment      | Marks the thread at the cursor done, or open again: the mark is on its first comment, and its answers show resolved with it |
| Delete Comment       | Takes the comment at the cursor away with its answers (an answer alone, when it is one): its range, its reference and the comment; the runs a range split stay split, as in Word |

What is not edited says why when it is tried: deleted text of a
tracked change, a field's result, a note's mark, a check box, text
drawn two ways in the file (=mc:AlternateContent=), a paragraph of a
text box, an edit across a table's cells, a join across a section
break, and every edit of a document whose protection is enforced (a
document protected for comments takes comments, as in Word). A comment
in a header or a footer is refused, as Word keeps none there.

* What is written

Only the paragraphs an edit touched are written again; every other
byte of the part, and every other entry of the ZIP archive with its
local header, is copied as it was. A save without edits returns the
file unchanged. Nothing is written that ECMA-376 does not define: no
=rsid= invented, no property Word would not write. A comment added
to a document without comments adds the comments part, with its
relationship and content type; an answer adds the extended comments
part (=commentsExtended.xml=) the same way when there is none, and its
entry names the answered comment's last paragraph by its =w14:paraId=,
which is given one when it has none. Resolving writes =w15:done= in
that part, made the same way. Deleting a comment takes its entries out
of the parts Word keeps beside the comments (=commentsExtended.xml=,
=commentsIds.xml=, =commentsExtensible.xml=); the comments part stays
when its last comment goes. A document opened with a
password is saved encrypted again with it.

* Export and conversion

None. Save As writes the document in its own format only; another
format is refused, saying so.

* Known differences

- No pages: the layout is Word's web layout, without pages, columns
  or the page's size.
- Only the first section's header and footer are shown, once.
- Pictures are not drawn: each is an object character in its
  paragraph.
- Comments are highlighted in the text, not shown in a margin.
- Ordinal and cardinal list labels are in English whatever the
  document's language.
- LibreOffice drops a paragraph mark inserted as a tracked change when
  it saves the document again; Word keeps it.
- LibreOffice 7.3 shows an answer as a comment of its own: it reads no
  threads. Word shows it under the comment it answers.

* Not implemented

- Section breaks are not marked in the text.
- Formatting edits (bold, styles, lists), and pictures, links, tables
  and notes inserted.
- Text boxes edited.
- Word opening every edited file without a repair prompt, checked by
  hand.
- Pages and printing.

* Limits and performance

Measured on 2026-10-09 on an Apple M1 Max, with the plugin's library in
a release build: a document of 20,000 paragraphs (4.4 MB of
=document.xml=) is opened and laid out as blocks in 0.18 s in 82 MB;
an edit there and the save take 0.17 s. The component is 1.0 MB as
released, its functions' names kept for backtraces. Kalem
fetches a document's items from it 2,048 at a time; its layout of a
document that large in the editors is not measured yet.
